01Context

Ad-tech campaign, sales, and finance data was spread across 12+ partner sources: REST APIs, files on S3, and exports arriving as Gmail attachments, each in its own shape and schedule.

The account management team pulled partner data from email by hand, aggregated it, and built client progress, final, and wrap reports, work that took hours to days. Finance ran month-end billing off the same manual exports. There was no shared warehouse and no application on top of it. I built both.

02Data Pipeline Platform
System 1 / ETL & ELT

A production platform that ingests from 12+ sources into an Amazon Redshift warehouse holding 400M+ rows across 6 years and 54 columns, and keeps it current on unattended nightly runs.

A modular ingestion framework

A connector → processor → enrichment → loader pipeline with fetch-first idempotency, retry with backoff, and memory-safe chunked bulk loading, so nightly jobs run without a human watching them.

Unattended nightly ingestion across 12+ partner sources

Fault-tolerant historical backfills

Ran large backfills (34M+ rows) at per-day granularity with fault tolerance, so a failure resumes instead of restarting and API rate limits are respected.

6 years of history loaded without tripping partner rate limits

Nightly NetSuite ELT

A SuiteAnalytics ODBC → Redshift job on a systemd timer loading 12+ raw tables, with certified SQL views validated against source exports so downstream numbers match the system of record.

NetSuite financials in the warehouse, reconciled to source

Anomaly detection and observability

A data-anomaly detection and correction framework (fix-at-source vs. override) with an append-only audit ledger, plus CloudWatch logging and threshold alarms. Diagnosed Redshift saturation and specified COPY-from-S3 staging and sortkey optimizations.

Corrections are traceable; saturation was measured, not guessed
About 10 pipelines serve account management (hours-to-days of manual work, now retrieved in seconds); 2 serve finance for month-end billing (days to hours). Controller review stays manual on purpose, since carry-forward adjustments need human judgment.
03Full-Stack Analytics Platform
System 2 / FastAPI + React on AWS

A FastAPI and React/TypeScript application live on a public HTTPS domain for about 60 internal users. I provisioned and secured the full cloud stack: RDS PostgreSQL, EC2 (ARM Graviton), an Application Load Balancer, ACM/TLS, S3, ECR-based Docker deployment, and DNS. 04Hot / cold two-tier architecture

The app can't query a 400M+ row Redshift warehouse live, but it does need current data. So I split serving from storage. The cold tier is the full warehouse in Redshift. The hot tier is the most recent year (about 58M rows) mirrored into PostgreSQL RDS, which serves live application queries.

The naive way to keep the hot tier fresh, a direct row-by-row Redshift to PostgreSQL transfer, benchmarked at roughly 1 hour per partner per month, which extrapolates to about 24 hours for a single year across 7+ partners. That was a non-starter for a nightly job.

Instead of row-by-row inserts, I built Redshift UNLOAD → compressed Parquet in S3 → DuckDB bulk load into PostgreSQL, replacing millions of individual inserts with a parallelized columnar export and load.

On top of that I added incremental sync by content fingerprinting. Data is partitioned by month in S3, and each monthly partition carries a hash fingerprint. Each nightly run compares fingerprints and re-pulls only the months whose data actually changed, swapping partitions atomically and dropping stale data. Fingerprinting takes about 10 seconds per month and a full month re-pull about 50 to 60 seconds, so the worst case (all 12 months changed) is under 15 minutes and a typical night is under 5, against the ~24 hour baseline. That is roughly a 99% reduction.

Monthly granularity was a deliberate choice, matched to how the data actually mutates.

Partners restate recent figures (impressions get revised days later from timezone lag or partner-side outages), which I handle upstream with a rolling 30-day re-pull into Redshift. After about 30 days the data is effectively immutable, so only the most recent month or two ever have changed fingerprints.

Fingerprints also catch my own corrections, like fixing mislabeled campaign names surfaced by anomaly detection, which can touch any month. That makes the serving layer self-healing regardless of where or why the data changed.

05Impact

Account management retrieves partner reports on demand in seconds instead of assembling them by hand over hours to days. Finance month-end billing prep dropped from days to hours. The serving layer stays current on a nightly job that runs in minutes, and the whole stack runs in production for about 60 users.
