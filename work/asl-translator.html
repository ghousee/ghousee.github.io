01The idea

Two communication barriers, one tool. People who sign and people who don't share no common channel; people who speak different languages don't either. I wanted one app that bridges both: read a hand sign or hear a sentence, and get the meaning back in a form the other person understands.

The interesting engineering is the glue, making a vision model and three APIs feel like one fluent tool.
02How it flows
03What I built

Real-time ASL recognition

Trained a CNN in Keras on an ASL dataset, then ran it live: OpenCV grabs a region of interest from the webcam, grayscales and thresholds it, and the model predicts the sign frame-by-frame.

A hand sign becomes text, live from a webcam

An AI speech translator

Chained three Google services: Speech Recognition (speech → text), Translate (100+ languages), and gTTS (text → spoken audio), with pygame for playback, so a spoken sentence comes back translated and read aloud.

Speak in one language, hear it in another

One desktop app to hold it together

A Tkinter GUI ties the modalities into a single tool with language selection and live status, handling API errors gracefully (unrecognized audio, failed requests) rather than crashing.

A coherent product, not a pile of scripts
04What it shows

This is the project where I integrated a trained ML model, computer vision, and third-party APIs into one working application, and handled the unglamorous parts (real-time capture, error states, audio I/O) that decide whether a demo is usable.

What I'd do next: expand ASL recognition from letters to full words/phrases with a temporal model, and move the heavy inference behind a small service so the UI stays responsive.
